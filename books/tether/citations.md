## Scope note

Tether is a fill-in journal. The prompts themselves make no factual claims — they ask questions.
Research appears in exactly one place: the front-matter essay **"The Tether,"** which explains why
the journal is built the way it is.

That makes the citation surface small and the standard correspondingly strict. Three research
claims appear in the book, and all three are traceable to primary sources below. Everything else in
the front and back matter is written as plain assertion, invitation, or instruction — not as fact —
and is phrased so a reader cannot mistake it for a research finding.

**No claim in this book promises the reader an outcome.** We say what researchers found. We do not
say what will happen to the reader's relationship.

## Entries

### [C-001] Appreciation in couples is linked to responsiveness and staying together
- **Claim:** In a multi-method series of studies, people who felt more appreciated by a romantic
  partner reported being more appreciative in return; more appreciative partners reported being
  more responsive to their partner's needs, were more committed, and were more likely to still be
  in the relationship over time.
- **Source:** "To have and to hold: gratitude promotes relationship maintenance in intimate bonds"
- **Author / Publisher:** Gordon, A. M., Impett, E. A., Kogan, A., Oveis, C., & Keltner, D. /
  *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 103(2), 257–274
- **Date:** 2012-08 (online 2012-05-28)
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22642482/
- **DOI:** 10.1037/a0028723 · **PMID:** 22642482
- **Tier:** primary
- **Accessed:** 2026-07-24
- **Confidence:** high — peer-reviewed; abstract read in full on PubMed; cross-sectional, daily
  experience, observational, and longitudinal methods across three studies.
- **Cross-check:** [C-002] independently supports the expression→relationship-quality link.
- **Used in:** front-matter ("The Tether")
- **Notes:** Correlational and longitudinal, not a randomized intervention. It supports *"feeling
  appreciated and being appreciative travel together with commitment"* — it does **not** support
  "gratitude causes relationships to last." The manuscript must not overstate this.

### [C-002] Expressing gratitude out loud predicts relationship quality six months later
- **Claim:** In a laboratory task where partners expressed gratitude to one another, the recipient's
  perception of the expresser's responsiveness predicted improvements in relationship quality six
  months later — an effect independent of two other emotionally significant interaction types.
- **Source:** "The social functions of the emotion of gratitude via expression"
- **Author / Publisher:** Algoe, S. B., Fredrickson, B. L., & Gable, S. L. / *Emotion*, 13(4),
  605–609
- **Date:** 2013
- **URL:** https://www.saraalgoe.com/publications
- **Tier:** primary
- **Accessed:** 2026-07-24
- **Confidence:** high — peer-reviewed; citation confirmed on the first author's own publications
  page; findings summarized consistently across independent sources.
- **Cross-check:** Consistent with [C-001] and with Algoe's find-remind-bind account [C-005].
- **Used in:** front-matter ("The Tether"), "How to Use This Journal" (the read-aloud step)
- **Notes:** The journal article itself sits behind a paywall; the abstract was read via
  secondary sources and the citation verified against the author's own listing. This is the
  citation for the **reveal-and-read-aloud** step specifically — saying it out loud is the part
  the research speaks to.

### [C-003] Writing privately about a relationship is linked to it lasting
- **Claim:** In a study of 86 dating couples, one partner in each couple wrote for 20 minutes a day
  for three consecutive days — either about their deepest thoughts and feelings about the
  relationship, or about daily activities. Couples in the relationship-writing condition were
  significantly more likely to still be together three months later, and both partners used more
  emotion words in their instant messages afterward.
- **Source:** "How do I love thee? Let me count the words: the social effects of expressive writing"
- **Author / Publisher:** Slatcher, R. B., & Pennebaker, J. W. / *Psychological Science*, 17(8),
  660–664
- **Date:** 2006
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16913946/
- **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01762.x · **PMID:** 16913946
- **Tier:** primary
- **Accessed:** 2026-07-24
- **Confidence:** high — peer-reviewed, randomized assignment to writing condition, abstract read
  in full on PubMed. Increases in positive emotion words partially mediated the effect.
- **Cross-check:** not required for the claim as stated; it is a single well-identified randomized
  study and is cited as such rather than as a general finding.
- **Used in:** front-matter ("The Tether") — this is the citation for the **write-apart** step.
- **Notes:** Sample was 86 *dating* couples, not married ones. Small n, 3-month follow-up, 2006.
  Twenty years old at publication — flagged for currency, and the manuscript cites it as one study
  rather than as settled science. **A widely-repeated "77% were still dating" figure appears in
  secondary coverage of this study but is not in the PubMed abstract; it is logged separately as
  [C-006] and is NOT used in the book.**

### [C-005] Find-remind-bind: gratitude's function in relationships
- **Claim:** Algoe's "find, remind, and bind" account holds that gratitude functions to alert a
  person to a responsive relationship partner, remind them of that partner's value, and strengthen
  the bond.
- **Source:** "Find, remind, and bind: The functions of gratitude in everyday relationships"
- **Author / Publisher:** Algoe, S. B. / *Social and Personality Psychology Compass*, 6(6), 455–469
- **Date:** 2012
- **URL:** https://compass.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1751-9004.2012.00439.x
- **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1751-9004.2012.00439.x
- **Tier:** primary (theoretical review)
- **Accessed:** 2026-07-24
- **Confidence:** medium-high — the citation is verified against the author's own publications page
  and a UNC College of Arts & Sciences feature on her work
  (https://college.unc.edu/2023/11/gratitude-algoe/, Kim Spurr, 2023-11-13). The Wiley page returns
  403 to automated requests, so the full text was not read directly.
- **Used in:** background only — **not cited in the manuscript.** Held here as theoretical context
  for why the mechanic is shaped this way.
- **Notes:** A theoretical framework, not an experimental result. Would be a citation error to use
  it as evidence that anything *works*.
