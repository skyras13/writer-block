# Book Brief — Tether

- **Slug:** `tether`
- **Genre branch:** `journal` (a sub-branch of self-help — see `CLAUDE.md` §8.3)
- **Status:** approved
- **Created:** 2026-07-24

## 1. The one-sentence pitch

A 90-day daily gratitude journal for couples with one rule: you write your side without reading
your partner's, and then you read them to each other.

## 2. The promise

Not more romance. **More attention.** Ninety days of practice at noticing your partner clearly
enough to name, in specific words, why you love them — and ninety occasions to say it out loud.

The promise is a practice, not an outcome. The book does not claim it will change a relationship.
It claims it will ask you, every day for three months, to look.

## 3. The reader

- **Who they are:** One partner in a committed relationship of three-plus years. Usually the one
  who buys the gift and notices when things have gone quiet. Late twenties to mid forties.
- **The situation they're in:** Nothing is wrong. That's why they haven't done anything about it.
  The reasons they love this person have become so obvious they've gone invisible. Evenings are two
  phones on the same couch.
- **What they've tried:** Date nights that got rescheduled. An app both of them stopped opening
  around day nine. A gratitude journal that moved from the nightstand to a shelf. Maybe a deck of
  conversation-starter cards that felt like an interview.
- **What they'd type into a search box:** `couples gratitude journal` · `journal for couples to do
  together` · `anniversary gift for him` · `relationship journal for two`
- **What makes them buy in 20 seconds:** The word **apart**. Every other product on that results
  page has two people writing on the same page, and this reader already knows how that goes — one
  writes, the other reads it and agrees. Private writing is instantly legible as the thing that
  makes it real. Then: three minutes. Then: it looks like an object worth keeping.
- **What would make them put it down at page 30:** Saccharine prompts. Therapy-speak. Anything
  answerable in one word. Anything they already answered on Tuesday.

## 4. The transformation

| stage | what they believe | what they do | what they fear |
|-------|-------------------|--------------|----------------|
| Before | "We're fine. I just don't say it much." | Notice things and let them pass unspoken | That "fine" is the beginning of something quieter |
| Early (wk 1–4) | "There's more here than I was seeing." | Look for one small thing a day, and write it down | Running out of things to say by week two |
| Middle (wk 5–9) | "Some of this is hard to say out loud." | Name repair, history, and shared labor — not just the sweet parts | Being the only one taking it seriously |
| Late (wk 10–13) | "I know how to say this now." | Say the harder things: forgiveness, wanting, what's ahead | Stopping when the book ends |
| After | "Noticing is a thing I do." | Keep noticing without a prompt | — |

## 5. The niche and the differentiator

- **Niche:** A daily couples gratitude journal with a private write-then-reveal ritual, arranged as
  a 13-week thematic arc. (From `market/recommendation.md`.)
- **The differentiator — a mechanic, not a tone:** **Write apart, reveal together.** Each day is a
  two-page facing spread sharing one prompt; each partner keeps one side for the whole book;
  neither reads the other's until both have written. No comp in the scan separates writing from
  sharing.
- **Comps we differentiate from:** *The Couple's Gratitude Journal* (Godkin — 5-minute shared
  format) · *Our Gratitude Journal: 52 Weeks* (Kusi — weekly) · *Gratitude Journal for Couples*
  (Simply Zen — 60 days, explicitly "together," 8.5×11).
- **What we are deliberately not doing:** Not a therapy or repair product. Not a two-copy
  long-distance set. Not a conversation-starter deck. Not competing on price with free printable
  worksheets.
- **Positioning line:** *You already know why you love them. Ninety days to say it out loud.*

## 6. Scope and shape

- **Format:** Paperback fill-in journal, print-first. (An ebook edition is possible but structurally
  compromised — see §9.)
- **Trim:** 6 × 9. Keepsake proportions, deliberately not the 8.5 × 11 workbook look.
- **Target length:** ~215 pages · 90 daily spreads + 13 weekly pages + front/back matter
- **Undated / evergreen.** Start any day; skipping a week costs nothing.
- **Structure:** 13 themed weeks. Weeks 1–12 hold 7 prompts each; week 13 holds 6. Total 90.
- **Front matter:** title page · copyright/legal · epigraph · "The Tether" (why this journal
  exists) · "How to Use This Journal" · "Before You Begin" (both names, start date, an intention)
- **Back matter:** "Keep noticing" · "A keepsake" (the app connection) · imprint page
- **Out of scope:** cover artwork · a long-distance two-copy edition · a family edition (that's
  book #2) · any clinical or outcome claim

## 7. Evidence posture

The prompts assert nothing — they ask. Research appears in exactly one place: the front-matter
essay "The Tether," which explains why the book is built this way.

Three claims, three primary sources:
- Appreciation travels with responsiveness and commitment [C-001]
- Expressing gratitude *aloud* predicts relationship quality six months on [C-002] — this is the
  citation for the **reveal** step
- Writing privately about a relationship is linked to it lasting [C-003] — this is the citation for
  the **write-apart** step

**Where the evidence is thin, and how we handle it:** [C-003] is one study, n=86, dating couples,
2006. [C-001] is correlational and longitudinal, not an intervention. Neither supports "this book
will improve your relationship," and the manuscript never says so. It names each study, gives its
size, and lets the reader weigh it. Two famous figures were **cut** for being untraceable — the
Gottman 86%/33% bids statistic and a "77% still dating" number attached to [C-003]. Both are logged
in `citations.md` with the reason.

## 8. Success criteria

The final QA pass is scored against these.

1. **Ninety distinct prompts.** No two answerable the same way, within a theme or across the book.
   Every one unanswerable in a single word.
2. **The arc is legible.** A reader on day 80 is doing something recognizably harder and deeper
   than on day 3, without being told so.
3. **Every research claim in the front matter is cited and correctly scaled** — no causal
   overstatement, no laundered figures, no promised outcomes.
4. **The voice holds for all 90 prompts.** Warm, plain, concrete, second person. No saccharine, no
   therapy-speak, no AI tells, no cliché.
5. **The interior is genuinely print-ready:** 6 × 9, mirrored margins with a real gutter, running
   heads, ruled write-in areas, facing-page spreads that fall correctly (One on verso, Two on
   recto), and no orphaned prompt.
6. **Non-assuming language throughout** — they/them for the partner in all 90 prompts, no gendered
   role language anywhere.

## 9. Risks

| risk | likelihood | mitigation |
|------|-----------|------------|
| The private step reads as friction and buyers pick the easier product | **medium-high** | Lead with the payoff, not the rule: "find out what they noticed that you missed." Keep each side to ~3 minutes. Accept that we lose the lowest-effort buyer — that's the bet, stated plainly in `recommendation.md`. |
| Prompts drift saccharine | medium | Voice-spec ban list + Editor pass on every single prompt line. A weak prompt is a defect, not a rounding error. |
| Two prompts in a theme collapse into each other | medium | Storyboarder's repetition audit; QA re-checks all 90. |
| Facing-page spreads misalign in the PDF (One lands on recto) | medium | The interior generator controls pagination directly; verified page-by-page after build, not assumed. |
| The category is crowded and we're invisible | high | Compete on mechanic, not keywords. Subtitle carries the search terms; the mechanic carries the click. |
| Reader quits around day 12, like the app that got abandoned | medium | The themed arc gives each week a new shape. Undated pages mean skipping isn't failing. |
| An ebook edition undercuts the product | — | A fill-in journal is a physical object. If an EPUB ships at all, it's a reading-only companion, and `PUBLISH.md` says so. |

## 10. Open questions for the human

1. **Author name as published?** Not set. Needed for the title page, copyright page, and KDP.
2. **Ebook edition — ship one or not?** Recommendation: **no**, or clearly labeled as a companion.
   Write-in space cannot survive reflow.
3. **Price point:** $13–17 band recommended. Where in it?
4. **Cover:** copy and design brief are in scope here; artwork is not. Want the brief?
