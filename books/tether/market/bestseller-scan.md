Therapist Aid, PsychPoint, and SimplePractice all publish free printable "Couple's Gratitude
Journal" worksheets aimed at clinical use.

AI-journal apps), all publishing large free prompt libraries as content marketing.
- **Read:** Prompts are commoditized. Prompt *sequence*, *voice*, and *physical ritual* are not.

## Gaps the market is leaving open

Each of these is verifiable from product structure, not from review sentiment.

1. **Nobody separates the writing from the sharing.** Every comp examined is either a shared page
   both partners write on, or an explicitly "together" activity. None has a private-then-reveal
   step. That is an unclaimed mechanic in a crowded category.

2. **Daily and weekly are treated as either/or.** The daily products have no weekly layer; the
   weekly product has no daily practice. A daily rhythm with a weekly synthesis is unoccupied.

3. **No product has a stated arc.** They are flat sequences of prompts. Nothing moves from easy
   noticing toward harder territory — repair, desire, the future. A reader on day 80 is answering
   the same kind of question as on day 3.

4. **Prompt repetition is a structural risk nobody has designed against.** Fixed-format products
   ("one thing you're grateful for, every night") ask the same question 60+ times. This is
   *inferred from the stated format*, not from reviews — but it is the obvious failure mode of a
   fixed daily template, and it is what a themed arc exists to solve.

5. **Free printables have collapsed the value of prompts alone.** Anything a therapist worksheet
   can do, the paid product must not be selling.

6. **Long-distance couples are unserved by "fill this out together."** A shared physical page
   assumes shared physical presence. Every comp assumes it.
