## b. Concept differentiation

| comp | what it does | what we do | material difference |
|------|--------------|-----------|---------------------|
| **The Couple's Gratitude Journal** (Godkin) | Daily 5-minute entries on a shared page | Daily entries written **privately on facing pages**, then revealed aloud | The private step. Godkin's product is collaborative-as-you-write; ours is deliberately not. |
| **Our Gratitude Journal: 52 Weeks** (Kusi) | 52 shared weekly entries over a year | 90 daily entries over 13 weeks, **plus** a weekly spoken reflection | Cadence is inverted: they are weekly-only; we are daily with a weekly layer. |
| **Gratitude Journal for Couples** (Simply Zen) | 60 days, 8.5×11, filled out **together** | 90 days, 6×9 keepsake, filled out **apart** | Opposite instruction, different trim class, different length. |
| **The Happy Couple Gratitude Journal** | Same two fixed questions every evening | 90 distinct prompts across 13 themes | Fixed template vs. a themed arc with no repeated prompt. |
| Free clinical worksheets (Therapist Aid, PsychPoint) | Printable open-ended gratitude sheets | A sequenced 90-day object with a ritual rule | Sequence, arc, and physical form. |

**Can we articulate a material difference from every close comp?** Yes — and in each case the
difference is *structural* (mechanic, cadence, arc, trim), not merely tonal. A tone-only
differentiation would be a finding worth flagging; this isn't one.

## c. Structural differentiation

- **Organizing device:** 13 themed weeks in a directed arc (ordinary → kindness → words → presence →
  choosing → history → shared labor → play → growth → repair → desire → outward → forward). No comp
  examined has any stated arc.
- **Page architecture:** two-page facing spread per day, one side per partner, held for the whole
  book. No comp uses facing-page partner assignment.
- **Weekly device:** "The Weekly Tether" — one shared prompt done aloud, on its own page. Original
  name, original device.
- **Framework name:** "noticing why." Ours.
- **Prompt set:** all 90 daily prompts and 13 weekly reflections were written for this book.

**Is our architecture a re-skin of any comp's?** No. The closest structural relative is a generic
daily journal, which is a category convention rather than any one author's design.

## d. Binding rules for the Writer

QA re-checks the manuscript against every one of these.

1. **Every idea in our own words.** No paraphrase that tracks a source's phrasing.
2. **Every borrowed fact, statistic, or quotation cited** via `citations.md`. Three claims in the
   front matter rest on research; all three are cited [C-001], [C-002], [C-003].
3. **Never reproduce another author's prompts, exercise lists, checklists, chapter structures, or
   framework names** — not verbatim, not lightly reworded. This is the highest-risk rule in this
   category, because prompt lists are what gets copied.
4. **Quotations short, marked, attributed.** The epigraph, if any, must be original or public
   domain with attribution.
5. **No invented reviews, endorsements, awards, or testimonials.** None appear in the book.
6. **No outcome promises.** We may say what the research found. We may not say what this book will
   do for the reader's relationship.
7. **Non-assuming language throughout.** They/them for the partner in all 90 prompts. No
   husband/wife, no he/she, no assumed marital status.
8. **No therapy-speak.** Not a style preference — it's what keeps the book from implying clinical
   competence we aren't claiming.

### Prompt-by-prompt originality check

All 90 daily prompts and 13 weekly reflections were written for Tether. Spot-checks against the
comps' visible sample pages and against the free worksheet layer found no overlapping phrasing.

Three prompts sit near category-standard territory and were checked with extra care:
- #37 "When did you first know? Tell it the way you'd tell a stranger." — *the "how did you meet"
  question is a category universal; the framing ("tell it the way you'd tell a stranger") is ours.*
- #64 "What do you need to thank them for that you never said out loud?" — *thematically common;
  this phrasing is ours.*
- #74 "Finish this: 'I still can't believe I get to…'" — *sentence-completion is a common device;
  this stem is ours.*

**Verdict on the prompt set:** original. A generic *theme* (gratitude, memory, repair) is not
ownable; a specific *phrasing* or *sequence* is what we must not take, and we haven't.

## f. Declared adaptation from our own prior work

The write-apart/reveal-together mechanic originates in **Tether**, the web app at
tether.skdaddle.com, owned by the author. The app describes itself as "A Shared Journal for Two"
and states its mechanic as: *"You write alone. When both of you have answered, the words unlock
side-by-side."* [C-004]

The print journal adapts that mechanic to paper. This is self-adaptation, not borrowing, and the
book's back matter names the app openly rather than presenting the mechanic as unrelated.

The 90 print prompts are **written for the book** and are not lifted from the app's prompt library.
