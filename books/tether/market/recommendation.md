## The niche

**A 90-day daily gratitude journal for couples, built on a write-apart / reveal-together ritual,
organized as a 13-week thematic arc.**

Not "a couples gratitude journal." A journal where the two of you answer the same question
*separately*, in your own handwriting, on facing pages — and then read them to each other.

## The reader persona

**Who:** One partner in a committed relationship of three years or more. Most often the one who
buys the gift, plans the anniversary, and notices when things have gone quiet. Late twenties to
mid forties.

**Their situation:** Nothing is wrong. That's the point, and it's why they haven't done anything
about it. They love this person and they've stopped saying why out loud, because the reasons have
become so obvious they've gone invisible. Evenings are phones on the same couch.

**What they've tried:** Date nights that got rescheduled. An app that both of them stopped opening
around day nine. A gratitude journal someone gave them that sat on the nightstand until it moved to
a shelf. Possibly a book of conversation-starter questions that felt like an interview.

**What they'd type into a search box:** `couples gratitude journal` · `journal for couples to do
together` · `anniversary gift for him` · `relationship journal for two` · `couples journal prompts`

**What makes them buy in twenty seconds on a product page:** The word *apart*. Every other product
on that page is two people writing on the same page, and this reader already knows what happens
then — one of them writes first and the other reads it and agrees. The private step is instantly
legible as *the thing that makes it real*. Second: "3 minutes." Third: it looks like an object
worth keeping.

**What would make them put it down at page 30:** Saccharine prompts. Therapy-speak. Any question
they could answer with one word. Any question they already answered on Tuesday.

## Why this one

1. **The mechanic is genuinely unclaimed.** Every comp in the scan is either a shared page or an
   explicitly "together" activity. Not one separates writing from sharing. In a category this
   crowded, an unclaimed structural mechanic is worth more than a better cover.

2. **Free printables have eaten the value of prompts alone** (scan §8). Therapist Aid gives away a
   couple's gratitude worksheet. A paid book has to sell something a printout can't: an object, a
   sequence with a direction, and a rule that turns writing into a ritual. All three of ours are in
   the mechanic and the arc.

3. **Nobody's product goes anywhere.** Every comp is a flat sequence. A reader on day 80 answers
   the same kind of question as on day 3. A 13-week arc that moves from noticing the ordinary →
   repair → desire → the future is a real reason to reach day 90 instead of quitting on day 12.

4. **Daily and weekly are treated as either/or in this market.** We do both: a three-minute daily
   entry and a spoken weekly reflection. That's the habit *and* the synthesis.

## The differentiator — a mechanic, not a tone

> **Write apart, then reveal together.**
>
> Each day is a two-page facing spread sharing one prompt. Left page is One, right page is Two.
> Each partner keeps one side for the whole book. You answer without reading theirs, then you
> reveal and read aloud.

Everything else — the voice, the arc, the trim — supports that one move. Writing apart is what
makes the reveal worth anything: you find out what they noticed that you missed entirely.

There is supporting evidence for this shape, and it's specific: writing privately about a
relationship for three days measurably improved relationship stability three months later
[C-003], and expressing gratitude *out loud* to a partner predicts relationship quality six months
on [C-002]. The book does both. We say this plainly in the front matter and we cite it; we do not
promise the reader an outcome.

## What we are deliberately not doing

- **Not serving long-distance couples as the primary market.** One physical book can't be in two
  cities. Real demand, real limit. It stays a keyword.
- **Not a therapy or repair product.** No clinical framing, no outcome claims, no therapy-speak.
- **Not a conversation-starter deck.** Questions get written down before they get spoken. That's
  the whole difference.
- **Not competing on price with free worksheets.** We're selling the object and the sequence.

## Positioning line

> **You already know why you love them. Ninety days to say it out loud.**

Supporting line for the listing:

> A daily couples gratitude journal with one rule: write your side without reading theirs — then
> read them to each other.

## Format implications

- **Trim:** 6 × 9 paperback. Keepsake proportions, not workbook proportions. Deliberately *not*
  the 8.5 × 11 that reads as a printable.
- **Length:** 90 days × 2 pages, plus 13 weekly pages and front/back matter ≈ **210–230 pages.**
- **Undated.** Start any day. No dead pages if you skip a week.
- **Price band:** $13–17. Above the printable-adjacent titles, below a gift hardcover.
- **Gift-ability is a design requirement**, not an afterthought: the "Before You Begin" page has
  space for both names, a start date, and an intention, so the giver can write in it.

## Content rules adopted from the niche map

1. **Never assume gender or role.** All 90 prompts use they/them for the partner. No husband/wife,
   no he/she. Costs nothing, serves more readers, and is already true of the Appendix A set.
2. **Every prompt must be unanswerable in one word.** A prompt that takes a single word is a defect.
3. **No two prompts within a theme may be answerable the same way.** The Storyboarder's repetition
   audit enforces this.
4. **Week 10 stays.** Repair and forgiveness belong in the arc even though "post-rupture" was
   rejected as positioning.

## The honest downside

The private step is friction. Every competing product is easier to use on the first night: open,
both write, done. Ours asks you to cover your side. If a buyer wants the lowest-effort option, they
will buy Godkin's, and they should.

Our bet is that the friction *is* the product — that the reveal is worth the rule, and that the
people who want a real ritual will prefer being asked for something. That bet is unproven. If the
book underperforms, this is the first thing to interrogate.
