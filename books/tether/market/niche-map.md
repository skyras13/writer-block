# Niche Map — Couples Gratitude Journals

**Date:** 2026-07-24

The head term ("couples gratitude journal") is crowded, commoditized at the free end, and
structurally homogeneous. We do not compete there. Below are ten specific pockets, ranked.

Demand and competition reads are **directional**, drawn from the structural scan and from what the
category's product descriptions target. Where a read is inference rather than observation, it says
so. None of these is a verified sales figure.

---

## 1. Couples who want a genuinely *daily* ritual — with a private step ⭐ RECOMMENDED
- **Angle:** Each partner answers the same prompt privately, then they reveal and read aloud.
- **Demand:** Strong. Daily-cadence products dominate the field, and one carries an Amazon
  "overall pick" badge with 800+ bought in a month (observed 2026-07-24) — the daily habit is what
  people are buying.
- **Competition:** Crowded on cadence, **empty on mechanic**. No comp examined separates writing
  from sharing.
- **Hook:** "Write apart. Reveal together. Ninety days of noticing why."
- **Risk:** The private step adds friction. Some buyers want the simplest possible thing, and
  "cover your side" is a rule to follow. Mitigated by keeping each side to ~3 minutes.

## 2. Long-distance couples
- **Angle:** Two people, two cities, one shared practice — answer the same prompt on the same day,
  reveal on a call.
- **Demand:** Real and underserved. Every comp assumes a shared page in a shared room.
- **Competition:** Essentially none in print. Apps serve this better today.
- **Hook:** "The same question, two hundred miles apart."
- **Risk:** A single physical book can't be in two places. Either you sell a two-copy set (higher
  price, more complex listing) or you accept that this segment mostly buys the app. **This is a
  real structural limit, not a solvable one.**
- **Verdict:** Keep as a *secondary keyword and gifting angle*, not the primary build.

## 3. Newlyweds / first-year couples
- **Angle:** A first-year ritual; the arc mirrors the first year of a marriage.
- **Demand:** Strong gifting occasion (weddings, showers).
- **Competition:** Moderate. "Married couples" framing already exists.
- **Hook:** "Start the habit while it's still easy."
- **Risk:** Narrows the buyer to a 12-month window and excludes long-married couples, who are the
  larger anniversary-gift market.

## 4. Anniversary gift buyers
- **Angle:** Positioned as the gift, not the practice. Keepsake object first.
- **Demand:** Very strong and seasonal.
- **Competition:** Heavy — every comp chases gifting.
- **Hook:** "A year from now, this is the thing you'll read out loud again."
- **Risk:** Gift-first positioning invites buyers who never open it. Good for sales, bad for
  reviews and word of mouth.
- **Verdict:** Use as a **listing and cover-copy angle**, not the book's spine.

## 5. Couples in or just out of counseling
- **Angle:** A structured between-sessions practice.
- **Demand:** Moderate.
- **Competition:** The free clinical worksheet layer owns this outright, and it's free.
- **Hook:** "Homework you'll actually look forward to."
- **Risk:** **Drags us toward therapy-speak** — the exact register the voice spec bans — and toward
  implied clinical claims we cannot support. Drop.

## 6. Couples with young kids
- **Angle:** Three minutes after the kids are down.
- **Demand:** Strong. Time scarcity is the real objection this segment has.
- **Competition:** Low as an explicit angle; "5 minutes" products imply it without naming it.
- **Hook:** "Three minutes, after the house goes quiet."
- **Risk:** Narrows the persona, and the content itself wouldn't change much. Better as a line in
  the description than a whole positioning.
- **Verdict:** Fold into the primary as a use-case, not a separate book.

## 7. Long-married couples (10+ years)
- **Angle:** For people who love each other and have stopped saying why.
- **Demand:** Moderate; overlaps heavily with anniversary gifting.
- **Competition:** Low as an explicit angle.
- **Hook:** "You already know why. When did you last say it out loud?"
- **Risk:** Hard to target with keywords — nobody searches "journal for long-married couples."
- **Verdict:** This is the emotional truth of the book. Use it in the copy; don't build the SEO on
  it.

## 8. Same-sex and queer couples
- **Angle:** A journal whose language never assumes a husband and a wife.
- **Demand:** Real, and chronically served by afterthought.
- **Competition:** Low.
- **Hook:** "Written without assuming who you are."
- **Risk:** As a *positioning*, it's a smaller market than the whole. As a **baseline requirement**,
  it costs nothing and improves the book for everyone.
- **Verdict:** **Adopt as a hard content rule, not a niche.** Every one of the 90 prompts uses
  "they/them" and never gendered role language. This is already true of the Appendix A prompt set.

## 9. Couples rebuilding after a rupture
- **Angle:** Repair-forward — a way back to noticing after something broke.
- **Demand:** Real but delicate.
- **Competition:** Low.
- **Hook:** "Start with what's still here."
- **Risk:** High. It implies therapeutic competence we're not claiming, and it makes an ordinary
  gratitude journal into a crisis product. Drop as positioning — but **keep Week 10 (Repair &
  Forgiveness) in the arc**, which serves these readers without promising them a rescue.

## 10. Families / parent-child gratitude
- **Angle:** Same mechanic, different pair.
- **Demand:** Moderate.
- **Competition:** Moderate.
- **Verdict:** Out of scope for book #1. This is **book #2** — the same structure, duplicated in
  the factory, with a new prompt set.

## What I'd drop and why

**#5 (post-counseling)** and **#9 (post-rupture)** both pull the book toward clinical framing.
They would force claims about outcomes we cannot cite, and they'd corrupt the voice. The evidence
we have supports "expressing appreciation is good for relationships" — it does not support "this
book will repair yours."

**#2 (long-distance)** is the one I most regret dropping. The demand is real and nobody serves it.
But a single physical book cannot be in two cities, and a two-copy set is a different product with
a different price and a harder listing. It stays a keyword.
