# Keep Noticing

You're at the end of a book, which is a strange place to be told the book wasn't the point.

Ninety days ago you probably thought this would be about being more grateful. It was about being
more accurate. Gratitude is easy and vague. *There, that, that is why* — that's specific, and
specific is harder, and specific is what they actually wanted to hear.

So here is the whole thing, now that you've practiced it:

Notice one true thing. Say it to them out loud.

That's it. That's what the ninety pages were for.

You don't need a book for that, which is good, because you've run out of one. Some couples buy a
second copy and start again with different answers. Some keep a note on their phone. Some just get
in the habit of turning toward the other person in the kitchen and saying the thing instead of
letting it go by.

What you shouldn't do is put this on a shelf and go back to assuming they know.

They know. They just haven't heard it since Tuesday.

# A Keepsake

Look back at day one. At what you wrote, and at what they wrote, and at how differently the two of
you saw the same week.

That's the part worth keeping. Not the good handwriting days — the ones where you noticed something
they didn't know you'd seen.

This journal grew out of **Tether**, a shared journal for two at *tether.skdaddle.com*, which works
the same way: you write alone, and when both of you have answered, the words unlock side by side
[C-004]. Same idea, different object. If you want to keep going after this book, or you're in two
different cities and can't share a page, that's where it lives.

But the paper version has one thing the app doesn't. In twenty years someone will open this and see
your actual handwriting on the left and theirs on the right, and know exactly what the two of you
were paying attention to in the spring of whatever year this was.

Put it somewhere you'll find it again.
